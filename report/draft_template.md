# Introduction {-}

Customer churn erodes a telecommunications operator's revenue. In the Kaggle Telco Customer Churn dataset, the 26.5% who left held 30.5% of monthly recurring revenue. Under an illustrative cost model, contacting everyone loses money, so retention offers must target likely leavers. This report asks whether these data, an attribute-only snapshot of 7,043 customers, support such targeting, and which learner to use.

The five Parts mirror the brief. Part 1 reviews methods and evaluates the data. Part 2 locates where churn concentrates. Part 3 builds pre-processing pipelines fitted only on training data, testing alternatives by cross-validation (CV). Part 4 uses nested CV to compare a logistic-regression baseline with a decision tree, a random forest, XGBoost, a multilayer perceptron (MLP) and an ensemble. It evaluates them by area under the receiver-operating-characteristic and precision–recall curves (ROC-AUC, PR-AUC). It then sets a profit-based threshold on out-of-fold (OOF) predictions: each customer is scored by a model fitted without them. Part 5 concludes and examines fairness, privacy and sustainability. One script, `run_all.py`, reruns the analysis from the raw data and rebuilds this report (Appendix A). No model significantly outperformed regularised logistic regression. The report therefore recommends it: the simplest competitive model, well calibrated and interpretable.

# Part 1: Feasibility study and data evaluation

Of 7,043 customers, 26.5% left within the observation window. Churners held 30.5% of monthly recurring revenue ($139,131 of $456,117, US dollars throughout), paying $74.44 a month against $61.27 for stayers. A 1% relative retention gain raises firm value by about 5% (Gupta, Lehmann and Stuart, 2004). Reichheld and Sasser (1990) report profit gains up to 85% from cutting defections by 5%, though from client cases, not a formal study. Under Part 4's illustrative cost model, contacting everyone breaks even only at a {{BREAKEVEN_ALL}}% save rate (working in Table C5). Contacting month-to-month fibre customers (55% churn) breaks even at {{BREAKEVEN_S1}}%. Targeting therefore pays if a model concentrates churners.

Table 1 compares candidate methods. First, no clear winner is expected. The Duke tournament found logistic regression and trees among the strongest methods, and methodology mattered too (Neslin et al., 2006). Careful preparation lets logistic regression rival complex learners on telecom data (Coussement, Lessmann and Verstraeten, 2017). Deep networks lose to boosting on medium-sized data with uninformative features like these (Grinsztajn, Oyallon and Varoquaux, 2022). Benchmarks on this dataset place tree ensembles at ROC-AUC 0.84–0.86 (Poudel, Pokharel and Timilsina, 2024). SyriaTel reached 0.93 with XGBoost on call-detail, complaint and call-graph features (Ahmad, Jafar and Aljoumaa, 2019). Since learners converge here (Part 4), richer data are the likelier lever. Second, evaluation should be profit-based (Verbraken, Verbeke and Baesens, 2013; Liu et al., 2024). A profit criterion reorders models across eleven telecom datasets (Verbeke et al., 2012). Third, in field experiments the highest-risk customers were not the most responsive to offers (Ascarza, 2018). Uplift modelling on randomised campaigns is therefore the eventual goal (Lemmens and Gupta, 2020; Verhelst et al., 2024).

The data are IBM's fictional 21-column sample of a Californian telco (IBM, 2019; BlastChar, no date). IBM describes the label as leaving within the last month and as quarterly status, so the horizon is uncertain. Only 11 total charges are blank. Usage, complaints, network quality, contact history and timestamps are absent. Their absence caps accuracy, prevents temporal validation and hides drift. Transfer to a UK operator is an assumption, given end-of-contract notifications and the 2025 ban on inflation-linked rises (Ofcom, 2026b). Gender, senior status and proxies such as payment method require a fairness audit. Verdict: feasible now as monthly batch scoring, with an analyst owning costs and fairness monitoring. Survival models need timestamps; uplift models need randomised campaigns. Success means hold-out profit above the best simple targeting rule.

# Part 3: Data pre-processing

A stratified 80/20 hold-out (seed 42) preceded every modelling choice, leaving 5,634 training and 1,409 test customers, 26.5% churners in each. Full-dataset pre-processing causes leakage and over-optimistic results (Kapoor and Narayanan, 2023). Every fitted step (imputation, scaling, encoding, resampling, calibration) therefore sits inside a scikit-learn `Pipeline` (Pedregosa et al., 2011), which cross-validation refits on training folds only. Options were compared by five-fold CV repeated twice on the training split, reporting PR-AUC as mean ± standard deviation (SD). A change was adopted only if its direction held across models and the corrected resampled t-test (Part 4) found it significant for at least one (Table 4). Pipeline choices drew on the same training split, so Part 4's nested-CV estimates are slightly optimistic; the test set is unaffected.

`TotalCharges` arrives as text because 11 rows are blank. All have zero tenure and none churned: they are unbilled customers whose true total is zero (Snippet 1). Constant and median imputation tie on CV (0.661 ± 0.018), so meaning decides: the median would assign $1,397 to customers who have paid nothing. Total charges track tenure × monthly charges (median ratio 1.00; 5th–95th percentiles 0.92–1.07). The 59 customers outside ±20% look like plan changes. No numeric field has interquartile-range outliers. The 22 attribute-level duplicates are distinct customers and were kept. `customerID` was dropped as signal-free and a privacy liability.

Six add-on columns and `MultipleLines` carry "No internet service" or "No phone service" levels that duplicate other columns. Encoding all three levels creates exactly collinear dummies (the dummy-variable trap), blurring odds ratios even under L2 regularisation. Trees are indifferent. Snippet 2 collapses these levels to "No". Nominal features are coded against reference categories (month-to-month, DSL, bank transfer) for the linear model; trees use `drop="if_binary"`. Collapsed and three-level encodings scored alike (Table 4), so parsimony decided. Ordinal `Contract` tied (0.661), and label-encoding nominal features would impose a false order on linear models.

**Snippet 2.** Collapsing redundant levels and choosing the encoder (`src/features.py`).

All pipelines standardise numeric features, which logistic regression and MLP need; trees are scale-invariant. `TotalCharges` is right-skewed (skewness 0.96; Figure C1). A log1p transform raised PR-AUC from 0.661 to 0.673 for logistic regression (p = 0.013) and from 0.649 to 0.664 for the MLP (p = 0.18), so both use it. Variance inflation factors (VIFs) were extreme for `MonthlyCharges` (860) and the internet-service dummies (148 and 103), because the monthly charge is almost a sum of service prices. Collinearity does not harm prediction: dropping or replacing `TotalCharges` cost 0.002–0.003 PR-AUC, so it was kept; dropping `MonthlyCharges` changed nothing (+0.0001, p = 0.74). It does blur coefficients, so odds ratios come from an interpretation model refitted without `MonthlyCharges`, whose VIFs are at most 6.5 (Table C2).

Snippet 3 builds six candidate features. Five were tested with XGBoost, retuned per feature set because its defaults overfit (Table C1): add-on count, protection flag, auto-pay flag, price change and average spend. Two were tested with logistic regression: average spend and a contract-by-internet interaction. PR-AUC changed by −0.0044 to −0.0001 (Figure 4a), never significantly (p ≥ 0.10), so the models use the raw attributes.

```python
def engineer_features(X: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Row-wise engineered features (no statistics learned from other rows)."""
    X = X.copy()
    X["n_addons"] = (X[ADDONS] == "Yes").sum(axis=1)
    X["any_protection"] = ((X[PROTECTION] == "Yes").any(axis=1)).astype(int)
    X["auto_pay"] = X["PaymentMethod"].str.contains("automatic").astype(int)
    tc = X["TotalCharges"].fillna(0.0)
    X["avg_monthly_spend"] = np.where(X["tenure"] > 0, tc / X["tenure"].clip(lower=1),
                                      X["MonthlyCharges"])
    X["price_change"] = X["MonthlyCharges"] - X["avg_monthly_spend"]
    X["contract_x_internet"] = X["Contract"] + " | " + X["InternetService"]
    return X
```

Four imbalance options were compared with the final logistic pipeline (Figure 4b): none, `class_weight="balanced"`, SMOTE (Chawla et al., 2002) and threshold moving. SMOTE ran inside an imbalanced-learn pipeline (Lemaître, Nogueira and Aridas, 2017), so only training folds were resampled (Snippet 4). Neither weighting nor SMOTE improved PR-AUC (0.672 and 0.671 against 0.673). Both raised recall (0.80 and 0.79 against 0.54) but damaged calibration, as van den Goorbergh et al. (2022) predict. The Brier score (Brier, 1950) rose from 0.133 to 0.162 and 0.160 (p < 0.001). The per-fold expected calibration error (ECE) rose from 0.025 to 0.146 and 0.137. Plain SMOTE ran on encoded columns; any resampling, SMOTE-NC included, shifts the learned base rate. Threshold moving reached recall 0.74 at per-fold F1-optimal thresholds (mean 0.33) without changing probabilities. A cost-sensitive decision needs calibrated probabilities (Elkan, 2001), so resampling was rejected.

# Part 4: Statistical and Advanced Modelling

Recommendation: use logistic regression and contact the riskiest 23.7% of customers (the best single threshold, 0.47, under illustrative costs). On the test set, expected profit was $8.26 per customer scored.

Baselines were a majority-class dummy and L2-regularised logistic regression. Advanced models were a pruned decision tree, random forest (Breiman, 2001), XGBoost (Chen and Guestrin, 2016), an MLP and a soft-voting ensemble of logistic regression, forest and XGBoost. Nested cross-validation evaluated each: a five-fold outer loop, repeated twice, around an inner randomised search (Appendix B). No configuration was scored on its own tuning data (Cawley and Talbot, 2010), Part 3's choices and the ensemble excepted. The search maximised average precision (PR-AUC), which suits ranking churners under imbalance better than ROC-AUC (Saito and Rehmsmeier, 2015). After selection, each model, retuned on the full training split, was scored once on the test set.

The majority baseline scores 73.5% test accuracy yet finds no churner (Table 5b). Tuned logistic regression leads the single models on nested-CV PR-AUC (0.672 ± 0.017; Table 5a, Figure 5). Tuning lifted the tree, forest and XGBoost by 0.248, 0.041 and 0.043 PR-AUC (all p < 0.001; Tables 6 and C1). It closed their train–validation gaps from 0.35–0.62 to 0.05–0.09 but left logistic regression and the MLP unchanged. XGBoost's chosen depth ranged from 1 to 8 across outer folds (100 draws; Table B2): its optimum is flat. On the test set the four strong single models reach ROC-AUC 0.847–0.849 (Figure 6) and the ensemble 0.849 (Table 5b).

No model significantly beats logistic regression (Table 6). On the outer folds, XGBoost, the forest and the MLP trail by 0.005–0.011 PR-AUC (p = 0.22–0.44, corrected resampled t-test; Nadeau and Bengio, 2003). The pruned tree is significantly worse (−0.048, p = 0.002); the ensemble adds 0.001 (p = 0.82). Non-significance over ten folds is not equivalence, but test-set bootstrap 95% intervals bound the three models' PR-AUC gaps within −0.023 to +0.016. DeLong's ROC-AUC test gives them p = 0.37–0.83 (DeLong, DeLong and Clarke-Pearson, 1988), and McNemar's test at the operating thresholds p = 0.15–0.48 (Dietterich, 1998).

Selection used nested-CV PR-AUC, but profit, the stated objective, can reverse accuracy rankings (Gómez-Vargas, Maldonado and Vairetti, 2025). Out of fold (Table 5a), the ensemble, MLP and XGBoost earn $202–$1,370 more expected profit than logistic regression; on test they earn $859–$1,121 less (Table 6). Every 95% interval includes zero. Only the pruned tree is significantly worse out of fold (−$3,932). Logistic regression was therefore kept, being cheaper, well calibrated and transparent: tuning a forest takes {{FIT_RATIO}} times longer. The whole modelling stage took {{MODEL_MINUTES}} minutes and {{CO2E_G}} g CO2e (CodeCarbon; Courty et al., 2024).

Thresholds were chosen on pooled out-of-fold training predictions; the F1- and F2-optimal values (0.35 and 0.17) ignore money. Under an illustrative cost model (contact $5; $60 offer to every contacted stayer and saved churner; 30% save rate; saved churner worth 40% margin on twelve months' charges), expected profit peaks at 0.47 (Figure 7b), staying within 5% of its maximum from 0.37 to 0.51; other save rates and offer costs move it between 0.17 and 0.75 (Figure C4). On the test set this threshold reaches 58% of churners at 65% precision (Figure 7a) and earns $11,645, 38% of the oracle's $30,587; contacting everyone loses $36,688, and the best segment rule on training data (all month-to-month fibre customers) earns $9,217 (Table C5). The F1 threshold changes profit by −$1,703 (95% CI −$3,468 to +$188; Table 6). Costs enter only at the decision, as cost-based decision rules generally outperform cost-sensitive training, and per-customer thresholds averaged best across nine datasets including this one (Vanderschueren et al., 2022). Contacting each customer whose expected profit is positive adds $1,382 out of fold and $127 on test (95% CI −$1,381 to +$1,739; Table C5): worth piloting.

Expected-value targeting needs honest probabilities (Van Calster et al., 2019). Logistic regression is well calibrated: pooled out-of-fold Brier 0.133, ECE 0.007 and slope 0.98 (0.97 on test; Figure 6c, Table C4). Recalibration was applied only where it cut out-of-fold Brier by over 0.001. XGBoost (slope 0.97), the forest (1.09) and the MLP (0.99) needed none. The pruned tree (slope 0.69) was recalibrated by isotonic regression inside cross-validation (Zadrozny and Elkan, 2002; Niculescu-Mizil and Caruana, 2005); on test its slope rose from 0.74 to 1.03.

Odds ratios (ORs) come from Part 3's interpretation model, refitted without monthly charges (every VIF at most 6.5; Table C2). ORs against reference levels include two-year contract 0.19 (95% CI 0.13–0.26), one-year 0.48, fibre 3.03, electronic cheque 1.41, online security 0.73 and technical support 0.77 (Figure 8a, Table C3). There is no evidence of an effect for gender (OR 1.02, 95% CI 0.90–1.17) or senior status (1.16, 0.93–1.43). Logistic regression's permutation importance (Breiman, 2001; Strobl et al., 2007) exceeds 0.01 test PR-AUC only for log total charges, internet service and contract (Figure 8c). XGBoost's SHAP values (Lundberg and Lee, 2017) rank month-to-month contract, tenure and fibre highest (Figures 8b and C5). Scoring-model coefficient rankings broadly agree with both (Spearman ρ = 0.75 and 0.60), except on collinear tenure, monthly charges and multiple lines. Importance is not causation.

Of the 157 churners missed, 46% had tenure above 24 months and 28% held one- or two-year contracts (Table C6). Their mean predicted probability is 0.27; every churner on a two-year contract or without internet is missed. False alarms are the mirror image: 98% month-to-month, 80% fibre, mean tenure 13 months. The learning curve (Figure C3) has flattened (validation PR-AUC 0.669 at n = 2,768 and 0.672 at n = 4,507; train–validation gap 0.007): more rows are unlikely to help, richer attributes might.

# Part 5: Conclusion and Ethics in AI

Logistic regression matched tuned boosting, forests and a neural network within 0.011 PR-AUC, so the simplest model was chosen: calibrated and interpretable. Sustainability agrees: sub-second quarterly retrains on minimised data are proportionate. The model passed Part 1's success test: $2,428 above the best segment rule on test (95% CI $260 to $4,525). Weaknesses: attribute-only data cap ROC-AUC near 0.85, all two-year-contract churners are missed and costs are illustrative.

Deployment should recompute the profit threshold from the operator's own costs, pilot per-customer targeting and log contacts to measure save rates. Timestamped behavioural data would allow drift monitoring under a model card (Mitchell et al., 2019) and datasheet (Gebru et al., 2021). Randomised campaigns would enable uplift modelling.

A flag allocates a benefit, a retention offer: a miss withholds it (miss rate); an unneeded flag adds contact (false-positive rate). Churners with dependents are missed at {{DEP_MISS_BASE}}% against {{NODEP_MISS}}% (Table 7, Figure 9), and partnered churners at 59% against 33%. Both lose offers, so both get the same remedy: a lower out-of-fold threshold. Together, thresholds of {{DEP_THR}} and 0.35 cut test miss rates to {{DEP_MISS}}% and 36%, costing $1,041 (Kozodoi, Jacob and Lessmann, 2022). Seniors are over-contacted instead: flagged 2.3 times as often, with 2.9 times the false-positive rate. With base rates of 44% against 23%, calibration and equalised odds cannot both hold (Kleinberg, Mullainathan and Raghavan, 2017). Contact caps, not age-aware thresholds that withhold offers, suit seniors. Gender shows no gap.

The Equality Act 2010 allows justification of age-based, not sex-based, treatment (s. 13(2)), so deployment should omit gender, with test PR-AUC unchanged (0.665; Table C7). Dropping senior status too still leaves 45% of seniors flagged against 22% of others (false-positive rate 0.27 against 0.12) and cuts expected profit from $11,645 to $11,062. Its proxies carry the gap: internet service, payment method and contract (Cramér's V 0.27, 0.20, 0.14; Barocas, Hardt and Narayanan, 2023).

A UK operator would rely on legitimate interests (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(f)), give transparency information (Articles 13–14), honour the absolute right to object to marketing profiling (Articles 21(2)–(3)) and complete an Article 35 impact assessment (ICO, 2023). With meaningful human review, retention offers are not solely automated "significant decisions" under Articles 22A–22D, inserted by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 from 5 February 2026 (SI 2026/82). Electronic offers need consent or the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003 soft opt-in. Obstructive cancellation could breach General Condition C1.8 (Ofcom, 2026a). Targeting likely leavers also taxes loyalty: two-year customers never see the offer, a mechanism behind the £4 billion loyalty penalty (Competition and Markets Authority, 2018).

A script checked every computed number against saved results, citations were checked against sources, and separate AI agents reviewed the AI-generated code, catching one since-corrected leak: a threshold chosen on test data. This honours BCS duties of due care and professional responsibility (BCS, 2022).
